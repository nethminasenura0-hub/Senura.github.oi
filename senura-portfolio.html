About Me

I work at the intersection of user experience and technical documentation — two disciplines most teams treat as separate, and that I've learned are actually the same job done at different stages. Leading UX means understanding what people need before they can articulate it. Leading research and documentation means making sure that understanding doesn't get lost the moment a project scales past one person's memory.

What drives me is the gap between a good idea and a usable product — the communication breakdowns, the undocumented decisions, the assumptions no one wrote down. I close that gap through clear communication up front, structured documentation throughout, and a genuine investment in making sure everyone — from engineers to end users — understands what we're building and why.

Featured Work

LAN Scanner & Active Port Scanner

NETWORKING · TOOLING
PROBLEM
Network administrators and security-conscious users often lack a fast, transparent way to see exactly what devices and open ports exist on their local network — a basic but critical blind spot for troubleshooting and security auditing.
SOLUTION
Designed and built a LAN Scanner and Active Port Scanner that discovers live hosts on a network and identifies open ports on each one, giving users a clear, structured view of their network's attack surface and connected devices — without relying on expensive enterprise tooling.
OUTCOME
A lightweight, practical utility that turns an opaque network into a readable map — reflecting how I approach every project: identify what's invisible, make it visible, and document it clearly enough that anyone can use it.
